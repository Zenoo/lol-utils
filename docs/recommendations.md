# Recommendation model

The engine contains no champion-to-build table, tier list, win rates, scraped builds, or champion-specific item IDs. Patch data is its input. General model weights and game rules are code: data-driven does not mean an absence of assumptions.

Completed progressions reserve an early slot for boots. Items explicitly hidden from client item sets are excluded even when their map flag claims Rift availability; otherwise the game can drop a numbered block. Ability text identifying empowered attacks increases attack synergy, Spellblade and repeated on-hit value. Published health ratios value melee cleave. Wounds parameters and descriptions identify healing reduction; armor plus reactive Wounds receives early priority against a known physical, sustaining lane opponent, with taunt improving trigger reliability. Later slots use the whole enemy team. Ally damage mix modestly adjusts offensive stat value. These are qualitative utility weights, not simulated combat damage.

The initial client import uses the selected champion and role. Once all ten champion IDs are present, the service refreshes only the item set using both complete teams; intermediate picks do not trigger refreshes. A changed complete roster or role assignment refreshes it again. Draft records carry no invented inventory or kill leads. Lane priority requires a reported opponent role; when the client omits enemy roles, guidance uses the team threat mix rather than claiming to know the matchup. The main preview uses this context for the client's selected champion as well. Automatic refresh respects the auto-import setting.

## Item scoring

1. Derive a champion profile from client tactical damage type, attack/cast style, durability, crowd control, mobility, roles, and ability descriptions. Do not treat unlabelled client spell coefficients as known AP/AD ratios.
2. Estimate threats from observable player records. Weight level, kill participation, deaths, and purchased item value; infer physical/magic threat from the champion profile. Estimate defenses from nonlinear base-stat growth and visible item stats. This does not reveal live enemy armor, hidden cooldowns, unspent gold, or positioning.
3. Price single-stat components from the current catalog to derive relative stat values. Parse omitted newer stats from the official `<stats>` block. Unknown stat prices use generic fallback weights, not invented patch statistics.
4. Score offensive, defensive, mobility, resource, and support stats against that profile. Adjust selected described effects for sustain, crowd control, attack frequency, and penetration. Total AP multiplication is approximated; most unique effects are not simulated.
5. Restrict purchases to the map, champion, role, and available summoner spells. Jungle companions require Smite; support starters require the support role, and selectable support rewards require their completed parent quest item. Automatic transformations and unrelated buff/ally-gated recipes remain unavailable as direct purchases. Extract game item tiers and shared group limits, including hashed group keys, to keep prismatic variants out of Classic builds and prevent illegal combinations. Map flags alone do not establish Classic-mode eligibility.
6. Credit owned ingredients as a multiset. Shared nested ingredients can only be credited once. Rank complete targets, then suggest affordable missing components if the target cannot yet be bought. Respect six inventory slots and label unaffordable targets as saving options. The 2–3 suggestions are alternatives, not a shopping basket to buy together.

Pregame item pages list starter alternatives separately, then greedily fill six completed slots by this scoring. They are a starting plan; live options use current game state. A score is a relative model utility per gold, not win probability or damage per second. Support utility increases with observed allied carry strength, and overlapping allied auras are discounted. Full ally composition optimization and damage-rotation simulation are not implemented. Published item passive parameters and calculation trees are archived; only selected formulas are evaluated.

## Runes and summoners

Rune scoring uses current official descriptor attributes, kit utility, and limited description matching. It chooses one perk from each primary slot, two from distinct secondary slots in an allowed second tree, and one stat shard from each current shard slot. It does not consume Riot's recommended pages or any external build source. Text matching is English-only and can miss conditional effects.

Summoner spells use current mode and level availability plus the client inventory. Flash placement follows the saved preference. A jungler requires an available monster-targeted spell; a missing legal pair aborts that import. Other slots rank damage, utility, protection, and mobility against the champion profile and role.

## Skills and uncertainty

Champion game records supply per-slot rank gates, including nonstandard six-rank basics. The decoder accounts for free starting ranks on transformation and attachment kits, automatically levelled slots, and script-controlled four-stance final ranks. Aphelios's passive parameters identify stat allocation; its guide compares marginal attack DPS from the published AD, AS, and penetration gains, labels the stat, and excludes automatically learned R from the point budget. Ability priorities use published cooldown changes, effect arrays, description utility, and ultimate availability. DDragon often publishes zero effect arrays and unresolved damage expressions. A generic priority can be strategically wrong even when legal.

Role quest effects are considered before ordinary core purchases. Jungle companion choices value their clearing/Smite progression and future burn, slow, speed, or protection. Their eventual bonuses are labelled as post-completion benefits; a shield without a published amount receives qualitative utility rather than an invented shield value. Support scoring includes published GP10 over a shared two-minute comparison horizon, regeneration, ward capacity, and whether the champion can trigger healing, shielding, ability-damage, attack, or crowd-control effects. Income valuation and qualitative utility weights are model assumptions. Quest counters and buffs are not exposed by the documented live API: support completion is established from the actual completed parent item, and the service remembers purchased quest items so consumed jungle companions are not repeatedly recommended.

A patch mismatch or an unavailable patch version produces an app warning and does not block recommendations or imports. The overlay receives only skill and purchase options, never patch warnings.

A future optimal-build claim would need complete per-rank formulas, condition/stack modeling, interactions, constraints, opponent scenarios, objective definitions, and empirical validation. This version deliberately does not make that claim.
